# Day-N playbook

## Look
Warm and close. A gradient sunset sky, a soft city or room behind, a darker floor, drifting dust and a vignette. Characters are the stars: large, outlined, with a blob shadow, standing in the lower half. Cards are cream with thick ink borders and a solid shadow.

## Palette
Sunset amber, dusk purple and slate for backgrounds; cream paper cards; one accent (the series colour, default marigold) used for the badge, counters, buttons and the winning-comment label. Text is ink on cream or cream with an ink stroke on the world.

## Type
Lilita One for the badge, headlines and numbers; Plus Jakarta Sans heavy for comments and body. Headlines 2 to 6 words. Badge always capitals: DAY 3.

## Series chrome
- DAY N badge top left from frame 2, never moves.
- Up to three counter chips top right, compact numbers (2,140), small label under each.
- Recap: a card with "Previously" and the one-line summary, 3 seconds at the open, skipped when scene 1 is already a recap layout.
- Decision card: avatar, @author, quoted comment, heart and a counting like total, plus the rule line. Label "Top comment decided".
- Cliffhanger: "To be continued", the open question, a pulsing accent pill with the ask, and "Day N+1 follows your top comment".

## Motion
Slow push-ins at 60 percent of normal strength. Cards rise and settle with a small spring; words in headlines land one by one. Characters breathe and bounce on speech. No whips, no shakes.

## Camera
Gentle. Push-in by default, a slow pan only for a change of place.

## Sound
Warm acoustic bed at about -22 LUFS, soft thumps for cards, a gentle pop when the decision card lands. No risers, no whistles. Silence is fine on the cliffhanger.

## Hooks
- Recap first, but make it a line a stranger can follow.
- The audience's own comment as the reason for today's work ("you decided").
- The problem in the first three seconds.
- Cliffhanger that offers two options and asks for one word.

## Series rules
One small real step per episode. Counters only from series.json, and they only go up when something real happened. Thank the commenter by their actual words. Keep the cast and place stable; the story, not the novelty, brings people back.
