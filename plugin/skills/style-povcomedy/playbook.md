# POV comedy playbook

## Look
Flat cartoon with a thick ink outline (6 px at 1080) on everything: characters, furniture, tags. One room for the whole video, drawn as flat colour fields with no gradients or texture. Adult proportions, big round head, thin tube arms, hands that gesture all the time. The camera never moves much: slow push-in on single shots, a bigger push on the punch.

## Characters
Two, always. Side A on the left facing right (tag orange `#ef5b3a`), side B on the right facing left (tag blue `#2f7fd8`). Different silhouettes: one `round` + one `tall`, or `bean` + `bean` with different hair and glasses/headphones/scarf. Faces swap in a 7-frame squash pop. Mouths flap with the voice. The listener stays deadpan until their moment.

## Palette
bg `#f6e7c8`, ink `#16161f`, accent `#ef5b3a`, side B `#2f7fd8`, paper `#ffffff`. Rooms use soft warm walls (peach, mint, salmon) so the two shirt colours pop.

## Type
Lilita One for tags and the question card, Plus Jakarta Sans 800 for the title card. The title card sits top centre on every shot ("POV" badge + the episode title, 2 lines max). Captions are small yellow with a black outline, lower third.

## Shot grammar
- Close on the speaker (the listener's shoulder at the edge): the default, about 70 % of beats.
- Wide two-shot: the opener and one mid-video beat.
- Split A / B panels: the contrast beat.
- Punch: push-in, burst, flash, reaction inset, then 0.5 s of quiet.
- Closing card: comment question over both characters.

## Sound
Dry voices only. No music, no ambience bed (the references have none). At most one or two soft library sfx (pop, a tiny whoosh on the punch). Nothing high-pitched.

## Hooks
A first line that is already dialogue and already slightly wrong ("One coffee, please. The biggest one you have."). The title card carries the premise; never explain it out loud.

## The comment question
Two answers that are both fine to pick ("Team Bucket / Team Tiny Cup", "Caller / Voice note"). Never "who is right".

## 10 original episode ideas
Each is two sides who are both right, a habit instead of a group, and a last-third punchline.

1. **Walking distance: Americans vs Europeans** (street). A: "It's only a ten-minute walk." B: "Great, let me get my keys." Punch: B: "You are driving to the mailbox?" A: "It's at the end of the driveway. It's far." CTA: "Walk or drive?"
2. **Ice in the glass: Americans vs Europeans** (cafe). A's cup is half ice, B's has two cubes. Punch: A: "Where is the rest of the ice?" B: "That was the ice." CTA: "Full ice or no ice?"
3. **"How are you?": Americans vs Brits** (office). A: "Amazing!!" B: "Not bad." A: "Not bad is bad?" Punch: B, deadpan: "Not bad is the best thing I have ever said." CTA: "Amazing or not bad?"
4. **It's 25 outside: Americans vs Europeans** (park). A in a puffy coat shivering, B in shorts. Fahrenheit vs Celsius. Punch: B: "Why are you wearing a coat in a heatwave?" A: "Because 25 is nothing!" The split shows a thermometer each. CTA: "Coat or shorts?"
5. **Emojis: Millennials vs Gen Z** (living-room). The thumbs-up: M: "I sent you a thumbs up!" G: "That felt like you were mad at me." Punch: G: "A skull means I loved it." M: "I thought somebody died." CTA: "Thumbs up or skull?"
6. **Dinner time: Early eaters vs Late eaters** (kitchen). A: "Dinner's at six." B: "Lunch just ended." Punch: B: "At ten at night, then we start the good part." A: "Of what? Sleep?" CTA: "Six o'clock or ten?"
7. **Slang: Millennials vs Gen Alpha** (living-room). M tries "no cap". G: "Please never say that." Punch: M: "I said it with respect." G: "You said it like a tax form." CTA: "Keep it or retire it?" (Keep it kind: both sides laugh at themselves.)
8. **Packing for the weekend: Overpackers vs Underpackers** (living-room). A: three suitcases, B: one pocket. Punch: B: "I wore the jacket and the jacket is my towel." A: "Sorry, you have a what?" CTA: "Suitcase or pocket?"
9. **Cooking: Grandma vs Granddaughter** (kitchen). "A pinch" vs "exactly 1.3 grams". Split: a spoon vs a scale. Punch: Grandma: "How do you know when it's done?" Granddaughter checks the app. Grandma smells it. "It's done." CTA: "Pinch or scale?"
10. **Alarm clocks: Morning people vs Snoozers** (office). A: "One alarm at five." B: "Nine alarms, starting at six." Punch: B: "Each alarm is a small door I walk through slowly." A: "That is a very long hallway." CTA: "One alarm or nine?"

## Writing checklist
- Every line is spoken by A or B; no narrator.
- The funniest line is in the last third and carries `punch: true` + `hold: 0.5`.
- One `split` beat, one `wide` opener, one or two `swap` beats.
- Both sides get a good point (one reason each).
- Last beat is a `cta` question with two friendly answers.
- Run `megaedit check`: it warns about sensitive words, missing punchline and a missing question.
